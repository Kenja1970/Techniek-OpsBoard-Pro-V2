## Sustained over-allocation is a schedule risk, not effort

A resource above roughly 110% of capacity is not simply "busy". The overload expresses itself as: multitasking (which multiplies cycle time across every item they touch), deferred reviews (which starve downstream stages), quality escapes, and eventually attrition. Utilisation above 100% for more than a sprint or two is a planning error being paid for by a person.

## Read utilisation as remaining work, not booked hours

Booked-hours utilisation flatters. Compute load as **remaining effort ÷ remaining weeks ÷ weekly capacity**. That reflects what is actually still owed, time-phased, and it moves when work finishes — which is the behaviour you want from a leading indicator.

## Recommended response, in order

1. **Rebalance** — move work to available capacity. Always check the bench first; relief capacity is usually sitting in the same register.
2. **Re-sequence** — if two assignments collide, one of them frequently has float. Moving it costs nothing.
3. **Subcontract or add a tool** — a subcontract crew or a paid analysis tool can absorb a peak without a permanent hire. Model it as a resource with its own cost and bill rate so the margin impact is visible before you commit.
4. **Extend dates through change control** — legitimate and honest when the first three are exhausted.
5. **Descope** — the last lever, and it belongs to the client conversation.

## Unassigned work

An active item with no responsible owner is not merely untracked — nobody will pull it. Assign a named lead (one person, accountable) even when several people contribute; shared ownership reliably becomes no ownership. Where the tool models a team with allocation percentages, the lead row *is* the accountable owner.

## Unestimated work

Work carrying no estimate is invisible to earned value, capacity, and forecast. It silently deflates BAC and makes utilisation look better than it is. A coarse estimate beats none — the purpose is to make the work visible to the arithmetic, not to be precise.
